A Commodity Biography

The Global Biography of
a Tube of Tretinoin

Following one small tube of medicine through raw-material sourcing, generic manufacturing, informal transport, and divergent national regulation — to see how globalisation is written into an everyday object.

I.

The Journey of the Tube

Six stages, six jurisdictions of value, risk and regulation — from a chemical plant to a bathroom bin.

Stage 1 — Upstream

Raw Material Production

The active ingredient — all-trans retinoic acid — is sourced through international commodity chains. China is frequently cited as a major supplier of the pharmaceutical inputs behind generic medicines worldwide.

Stage 2 — Assembly

Manufacturing in India

A generic drug manufacturer in Maharashtra formulates and packages the final product — though the API, excipients and packaging materials are frequently outsourced to separate international suppliers first.

Stage 3 — Point of Sale

Sale in India

Sold over the counter at pharmacy counters — cheap, easily accessible, and largely unregulated at the point of sale.

Stage 4 — Informal Transit

Personal Border Crossing

Carried informally from India to Germany in personal luggage — outside any commercial import channel, customs declaration, or supply-chain record.

Stage 5 — Re-entry into Regulation

Regulation in Germany

Prescription-only under German pharmaceutical law — legal use requires a doctor's consultation before the same molecule can be dispensed.

Stage 6 — Exit

End-of-Life

The empty tube — plastic, aluminium, paper — leaves personal use and enters a linear "production → distribution → consumption → disposal" cycle, with no circular alternative built in.

III.

Two Regulatory Worlds

The same molecule, governed by two entirely different logics of access.

Access-Oriented

India

  • Sold over the counter, no prescription needed
  • Low price point, wide informal availability
  • Regulatory emphasis on affordability and reach
  • Weaker point-of-sale oversight of use
vs
Safety-Oriented

Germany

  • Prescription-only (verschreibungspflichtig)
  • Mandatory doctor's consultation before use
  • Regulatory emphasis on clinical oversight
  • Higher cost and access threshold for patients

Neither system is a universal default. Healthcare access is shaped by each country's own historical, political and economic trajectory — not by a single global regulatory standard.
